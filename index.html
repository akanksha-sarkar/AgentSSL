AgentSSL: Can MLE Agents Leverage Unlabeled Data?

Ethan Lin*, Akanksha Sarkar*, Ziang Liu, Kristin Branson, Jennifer Sun
*Equal contribution
Paper (soon) arXiv (soon) Code
TL;DR MLE agents can discover strong programs that leverage unlabeled data, but reliable program selection remains the bottleneck.

Can agents reduce engineering and annotation cost at the same time?

MLE agent research is focused on reducing engineering cost, but relies on human-labeled data. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) research seeks to reduce annotation effort, but requires human engineering to deploy. We study whether AgentSSL can reduce both costs.

Abstract

Acquiring high-quality labeled data and engineering effective learning pipelines are two critical bottlenecks to applying machine learning systems in new domains. Although recent MLE agents have shown promise in automating pipeline design, their behavior has been studied almost exclusively in fully supervised settings. In many domains, however, labeled data is expensive while unlabeled data is abundant.

In this work, we study agents in the semi-supervised learning (SSL) regime by framing SSL as a program search problem and exploring how agents navigate this space under limited supervision. Our analysis focuses on three questions: (1) whether agents can effectively leverage unlabeled data, (2) how they leverage it and what strategies they discover, and (3) whether they can adapt these strategies to specific domains.

We find that agents can leverage and synthesize SSL strategies spanning decades of SSL research, achieving performance comparable to, and in some cases outperforming, established human-engineered SSL methods. We further validate these observations on real-world ecological datasets, highlighting both the challenges and opportunities of transferring agent-discovered strategies beyond controlled benchmarks.

Method

We frame SSL as a program search problem using LLM-based agents.

Overview of AgentSSL. Given a small labeled set and a larger unlabeled corpus, an LLM-based agent generates SSL programs that are evaluated using either unsupervised SSL metrics or noisy validation feedback, with the resulting fitness score guiding iterative refinement. We evaluate AgentSSL on both image classification SSL benchmarks and real-world ecological detection tasks.

Contributions

  • We introduce Agentic Semi-Supervised Learning (AgentSSL), formulating SSL as a structured program-search task for agents.
  • MLE agents autonomously discover strong SSL programs, competitive with and in several cases outperforming established SSL baselines.
  • A systematic analysis shows agents exploit unlabeled data, explore ideas across classical and modern SSL, and produce diverse, performant solutions.
  • We identify reliable program selection as an open problem, with an empirical analysis of existing proxy metrics.
  • We evaluate AgentSSL on real-world ecology detection tasks, showing both the potential and the challenges of reducing annotation cost in practice.

Results

Across datasets and shot settings, AgentSSL discovers programs that are competitive with strong SSL baselines. The best agent solution (Abest) is generally comparable to the best human-engineered SSL baseline (Hbest) and outperforms it on several datasets.

The gap between oracle and practical selection is the central challenge: when unsupervised selection metrics correlate well with test accuracy, AgentSSL keeps much of its performance; when they don't, it drops substantially. The bottleneck is not generating good SSL programs, but selecting among them under limited supervision.

How do agents use unlabeled data?

Agents explore a broad range of SSL paradigms and often combine several within one program (DTD, evolutionary search).

Real-world ecology

We evaluate AgentSSL on animal detection from two ecological pipelines, WildFin (fish) and BuckTales (antelope), against the domain experts' YOLOv8 pipelines using small labeled subsets.

On WildFin, agents outperform the low-label expert baseline and approach the full-data pipeline. On BuckTales, they only match the low-label baseline: the expert solution relies on very high-resolution inputs, while agents spend their budget on more complex tiling and SSL strategies.
